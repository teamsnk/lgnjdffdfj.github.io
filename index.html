01 Overview

People in Dhaka buy plants with good intentions, then lose them.

Not through indifference, but because nobody told them what a plant needs, when, or how urgently. Little Garden turns that gap into a calm daily routine: one task list, plain-language guidance, and a camera that answers “what plant is this?”.

02 Discover

Research & discovery

Three lenses: what the literature says, what the market offers, and what Dhaka residents told me directly.

Desk research

Why it matters

Indoor plants are linked to lower stress and better wellbeing (Bringslimark et al., 2009). Some species filter indoor air (NASA, 1989). Rooftop greening eases urban heat in Dhaka (Safayet et al., 2017).

Survey · n = 30

What people do

An online questionnaire covering plant ownership, motivations, air-pollution concern, care difficulties, plant loss and wanted features, completed by urban Dhaka residents.

Interviews · n = 5

Why they struggle

Semi-structured conversations with a student, an office worker, a homemaker, a freelancer and a small business owner. Different lives, one shared pattern.

Opportunities

  • Own the beginner. Blossom proves simplicity works, but it has no onboarding or local context.
  • Keep identification, drop the intimidation. PictureThis identifies well. The answer needs to lead somewhere: a routine.
  • Be local. Monsoon and winter care, balcony and rooftop categories, and a path to Bangla.
  • Stay free to start. Subscription gates were a repeated barrier for novices.

05 Define

Problem statement and How Might We

Urban residents in Dhaka who want to grow indoor or balcony plants lack the knowledge, consistency and confidence to care for them. They need a simple, locally relevant tool that gives reminders, plain-language guidance and visible progress.

How Might We

  1. How might we make watering reminders feel helpful rather than annoying?Fatema · reminders, tone
  2. How might we help a complete beginner feel confident the first time they open the app?Rehnuma · onboarding
  3. How might we show what needs care today without making the user search?Both · task-first Home
  4. How might we replace care jargon with words a beginner already knows?Rehnuma · content

What changed from the first flow

  • Account moved out of the way. Testing showed 2 of 5 participants unsure what to do first. A single “Get started” replaces a multi-step intro.
  • Reminders became a destination. “Today’s Task” earned its own tab after testing.
  • Seasonal guide merged into Care Guide. One content home, not two.
  • User dashboard folded into Home. Counts and progress live where people already look.
  • Scan added. The main discovery from usability round 2.
  • Labels simplified: “Plants”, “Add”, “Today” replace “My Plants”, “Add Plants”, “Today’s Task”, which wrapped and crowded the bar.

09 Deliver

High-fidelity UI

Eight connected screens, built on the system and tested with five participants. Each decision below traces back to research.

Screens shown above are the Term 2 prototype. The refined versions follow in the next section.

Design rationale

Task-first Home

Reminders were prioritised because forgetting was the largest named difficulty. Today’s Tasks sits directly under the counters so the answer to “what do I do now?” needs no navigation.

Bottom navigation

A bottom bar keeps core tasks within thumb reach on mobile, and the same five items in the same order reduce relearning.

Reassurance after action

Beginners hesitate without feedback. “Great, Monstera has been marked as watered” and “Your little garden is thriving” confirm the action and encourage the habit.

Plain-language care

“Water when top soil feels dry” beats “moderate hydration”. Beginners disengage from jargon, so every care field is short and visual.

Refined flow

Add Plant → Scan → Result → Tasks

The complete loop, rebuilt and interactive. Tap through the phone or use the steps. Try the low-confidence path.

What changed, and why

1

Result had a single answer

A 95% match left no room for a wrong guess. Now: top match plus two alternatives, and a distinct “couldn’t tell” state under 60%.

2

“Add to my plants” skipped location and name

The Plants tab filters by Indoor, Balcony, Rooftop, so adding without location produced orphan plants. Now: a one-step details sheet.

3

No bridge to tasks

Adding a plant didn’t say a watering task existed. Now: a confirmation that names the task and links to Today.

4

Camera missing states

No permission explanation, no loading, no cancel. Now: a permission pre-prompt, a progress state with cancel, and Upload / Manual as always-visible fallbacks.

5

ON / OFF toggles for done

“ON, Done” on a task card is ambiguous. Now: a labelled circular check with Undo.

6

Text and typos

“Temparature”, “Next Watered”, and three different profile names (Hannah, Aleena Khan, Hannah Mackay). Now: “Temperature”, “Next watering”, and one consistent Dhaka-appropriate user.

11 Inclusive design

Accessibility considerations

Checked against WCAG rather than assumed. The audit caught the Terracotta failure on its own, and then found more.

1.4.3 · 1.4.11

Contrast

Text 4.5:1 or better, UI borders 3:1 or better. Three failing pairs found and replaced (see Design system).

1.4.1

Not colour alone

Every status has an icon and a label. Task state is a check plus the word Done.

2.5.5 · 2.5.8

Touch targets

48px minimum for buttons, checks, tabs and chips in actionable areas. Above the 24px AA requirement and the 44px AAA target.

1.4.4 · 1.4.12

Readable type

16px body, 12px label floor. Layouts wrap, not clip, at larger system text sizes.

4.1.2 · 4.1.3

Screen readers

Status messages (“Pothos added”) announced politely. Icon buttons carry labels. Scan result reads species, confidence and alternatives in order.

3.3 · 2.2

Error recovery

Wrong match, denied camera and accidental completion all have a visible way back: Retake, Upload, Manual and Undo.

2.3.3

Motion

The scanning line and progress animation respect “reduce motion”, falling back to a static state with text.

Cognitive

Low cognitive load

One primary action per screen, one icon family, short jargon-free copy, and confirmation after every action.

Inclusion

Language and context

Bangla is a Should-have. Dark mode needs its own contrast pass, not just inverted colours.

Still to doA device-level screen-reader pass (TalkBack and VoiceOver), automated contrast checks in the design file, and testing with participants who use assistive technology.
12 What’s next

Future development

The groundwork is built: a confidence-and-correct pattern that extends to diagnosis. What remains is connecting it to a real model and testing at scale.

Next 0–3 months

  • Round 3 testing with Dhaka users
  • Bangla localisation and bilingual care content
  • Native build with real push notifications
  • Screen-reader pass on device

Then 3–9 months

  • Plant health diagnosis: photo in, condition and confidence out, same result layout
  • Weather- and monsoon-aware reminders
  • Pet- and child-safe plant flags, from the storyboard
  • Offline care guide

Later 9+ months

  • Home air-quality view linked to plants
  • Local nursery and plant sharing
  • Community, with moderation
  • Weekly progress recap
LimitationsFive-person rounds, a Figma prototype with no backend, and no real notifications, performance or long-term engagement data. Diagnosis needs a validated image-classification model this project could not build or verify.
13 Reflection

What I’d tell the next designer

  1. Say what research cannot say. Separating motivation (clean air) from barrier (care) kept the product honest.
  2. Audit your own system. Contrast checks caught three failing pairs I had originally called accessible.
  3. One testing comment can reshape a flow. P5 turned a single entry card into a complete Scan loop.
  4. Always give a way back. Retake, Upload, Manual and Undo mattered more than the model’s accuracy.
  5. Design for the person who lost a plant. Tone is a feature.
